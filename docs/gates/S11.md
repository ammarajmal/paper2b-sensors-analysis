 60 RPM (heave ratio camera/laser 0.04): the laser is in heave lock-in (2.48 mm at 1.431 Hz, 99% of its variance in that line); the camera shows a clean but small sinusoid (0.10 mm) at 1.38 Hz, all three cameras equal (0.104, 0.103, 0.107). Camera frequency 1.38 vs 1.43 Hz is itself a different response frequency, not a small shift.
 80 RPM (heave 8.6, pitch 0.08): reversed regimes. The camera is in heave lock-in (1.87 mm at 1.47 Hz, three cameras agree to 0.5%, amplitude decaying 3.0 -> 2.3 mm over the first 8 s, so non-stationary) while the laser heave is only 0.22 mm; the laser is in torsional lock-in (pitch 0.0142 rad at 3.106 Hz, with harmonics at 6.2 and 9.3 Hz, side sensor 1.75 mm) while the camera pitch is 0.0011 rad. Different regimes again, with no sign of a fault.
 110 RPM (heave 0.46, pitch 2.5): both instruments at low amplitude (laser 0.13 mm, camera 0.06 mm at f_h); the camera's largest peak is at 2.27 Hz (0.15 mm), not at f_h, and the laser has no such peak: line amplitude at f_h compares small residual lines (laser line holds 21% of its variance, camera 6%). Pitch is at the floor (1e-4 rad, equal to the broadband floor).
 140 RPM (heave 0.30, pitch 1.007): pitch agrees to 0.7% (camera 0.0149 rad, laser 0.0148, all three cameras within 2%): a good agreement at a strong torsional lock-in. Heave at f_h compares small lines (laser 0.31 mm, all cameras 0.092 mm, again three cameras within 2%). In the heave channel both instruments also see the torsion line: the laser SIDE sensor 1.95 mm and the offset-marker cameras cam1/cam2 1.98 mm at f_alpha, while the laser CENTRE sensor shows 0.04 mm and the near-axis cam3 0.09 mm. The same pattern at 160 RPM (side 2.92 vs cam1/cam2 3.30 mm; centre 0.12 vs cam3 0.11) and 200 RPM (side 3.79 vs 3.97; centre 0.29 vs cam3 0.30). So the geometry matches the owner's marker map (cam1/cam2 offset, cam3 near axis) and the laser centre sensor is near the axis.
